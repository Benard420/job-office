# Writing Style for Letters and Messages

Everything the office writes goes out under the user's name. It must sound like a capable person, not a template or a machine.

## Voice

- Warm, specific, confident, plain. Short sentences mixed with longer ones.
- First person, active voice. "I supported clients with..." not "Clients were supported..."
- Match the user's own voice. If they gave a sample letter, mirror its rhythm, vocabulary, and sign off. If they use regional expressions on purpose, keep them where they fit the audience.
- Match the spelling convention of the target country (UK, US, Canadian, Australian).

## Avoid

- Em dashes and en dashes. Use commas, full stops, or "and".
- Filler and cliches: "I am excited to apply", "I believe I would be a great fit", "dynamic", "passionate", "leverage", "synergy", "fast paced environment", "proven track record", "go above and beyond", "delve", "tapestry", "I am writing to express my interest".
- Repeating the job description back word for word.
- Claims not supported by the CV.
- Generic praise of the employer ("your esteemed organization").

## Cover letter shape (one page)

1. Header: name, role title, email, phone (international format), optional city.
2. Date, recipient block (Hiring Manager, unit or team, organisation, city, country).
3. Subject line: "Re: <Role>, <Unit> (Requisition <req>)".
4. Opening paragraph: the exact role, unit, and site, plus one concrete link between the user and this employer (past placement, same patient group, same product type, local knowledge, a shared value backed by evidence).
5. Two experience paragraphs: the most relevant roles, with duties that match the posting's top requirements. Use the employer's own terms where honest.
6. One paragraph on how they would approach the people or work in this specific unit or team.
7. Practical paragraph: certificates, availability that matches the posted shifts, start date, relocation or authorization status stated plainly and positively, willingness to complete checks.
8. Short thank you and sign off.

Target 330 to 420 words. Never more than one page.

## Tailoring checklist (run before saving each letter)

- [ ] Employer, site, unit, and requisition number exactly as posted
- [ ] Role title exactly as posted
- [ ] Hours or FTE and shifts match the posting
- [ ] Every claim traceable to the CV or the user's answers
- [ ] At least two requirements from the posting addressed with evidence
- [ ] No banned phrases, no dashes
- [ ] Reads naturally aloud
- [ ] One page in the rendered PDF

## Cold email

- Subject: specific and short. "Health Care Aide application, B5 Medicine (413449)".
- 90 to 140 words. Who they are, what they applied for, one proof point, one clear ask (a short call, or to pass the application to the hiring manager), thanks.
- Plain text, no attachments unless the contact asked; offer to send the CV.

## Cold text or LinkedIn note

- Under 300 characters for LinkedIn connection notes; under 450 for SMS or WhatsApp.
- Name, role applied for, one line of value, one ask.

## Follow up

- Day 5 to 7 after applying or emailing, then once more around day 12 to 14. Stop after two follow ups unless the contact replies.
- Each follow up adds something new (a relevant certificate, availability change, a short portfolio link).

## Thank you note after interview

- Within 24 hours. 80 to 150 words. Thank them, recall one specific moment from the conversation, restate fit in one line, close warmly.
